About

Uncovering the hidden order in atomic nuclei

My work asks how simple organizing principles emerge from the complex interactions of protons and neutrons. I pursue that question through representation theory, symmetry-adapted ab initio modeling, high-performance computing, and machine learning.

I am the main developer of the Symmetry Adapted Model (SAM), have built general U(3) and U(4) coupling algorithms for nuclear theory, and created the Wigner-Informed Neural Network (WINN) to connect first-principles symmetry structure with global nuclear data. My long-term goal is a research program in which physics and AI continually sharpen one another: theory supplies interpretable structure, while AI guides discovery across the nuclear chart.

What I do

A connected program in symmetry, computation & AI

Σ

Symmetry-adapted ab initio models

Developing SAM as a high-performance framework for symmetry-resolved first-principles calculations, including three-nucleon interactions and extensions from light toward medium-mass nuclei.

⌘

Interpretable ML for nuclear systematics

Using WINN, equivariant architectures, neural quantum states, and active learning to discover, explain, and represent emergent nuclear symmetries.

⬡

Scientific leadership & collaboration

Leading interdisciplinary projects, an equivariant-learning lecture series, and scientific programs that connect nuclear theory, AI, and computation.
