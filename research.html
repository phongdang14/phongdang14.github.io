Research

Uncovering the hidden order in atomic nuclei

Discovering, explaining, and representing emergent nuclear symmetries—from first-principles many-body theory to interpretable and equivariant AI.

Representation theoryAb initio many-body physicsHigh-performance computingInterpretable & equivariant AI

Atomic nuclei are quantum many-body systems of rapidly growing complexity, yet their low-energy behavior often displays striking simplicity: collective motion, clustering, deformation, and spin-isospin regularities. My research asks how these patterns emerge from realistic interactions and how their underlying U(3) and U(4) symmetries can be used to organize calculations.

I combine representation theory, symmetry-adapted ab initio modeling, high-performance computing, and machine learning. The unifying goal is not simply to predict nuclear observables, but to build methods that reveal why the predictions work—and use that physical structure to guide the next calculation.

Focus areas

Three research threads

Σ

Symmetry-adapted ab initio models

Building SAM, a high-performance framework that exposes U(3) and U(4) content in first-principles wave functions and uses it to compress nuclear many-body spaces.

⌘

Interpretable & equivariant AI

Connecting symmetry-resolved calculations to global nuclear data with WINN, while developing symmetry-aware neural quantum states and exact-to-approximate equivariant architectures.

∮

Autonomous, physics-guided discovery

Developing uncertainty-aware surrogates and agentic workflows that select, run, validate, and interpret informative calculations across the nuclear chart.

Beyond the science, I invest heavily in building scientific communities — founding chapters, leading outreach, and advocating for researchers.

Selected projects

What I've been building

A progression from mathematical infrastructure to first-principles simulation and symmetry-aware AI—each project supplies a layer of the broader discovery program.

Wigner-Informed Neural Network (WINN)

Initiator & project lead · 2026–present

An intrinsically interpretable model connecting first-principles symmetry information to global nuclear masses, with accurate predictions, reduced data requirements, and stable extrapolations.

Representation Theory & Equivariant Neural Networks Lecture Series

Lecture-series lead · 2026–present

Leading an open LSU AI Journal Club series that develops the mathematical foundations of symmetry and connects them to practical equivariant-learning architectures.

Lecture materials ↗

Neural Quantum States & Equivariant Architectures

Project lead · 2025–present

Benchmarking neural representations of nuclear wave functions and extending them toward architectures whose hidden channels and couplings encode exact or approximate physical symmetries.

Deuteron benchmark code ↗

Ab initio Symmetry-Adapted Model of Atomic Nuclei

Co-investigator, main developer · 2024–present

Main developer of the theoretical framework and HPC C++ software. Current work incorporates three-nucleon interactions through the normal-ordered two-body approximation and extends systematic symmetry-resolved studies toward selected sd-shell nuclei.

Physics Quest 2025 — American Physical Society

LSU team leader · 2024–2025

Led the LSU team in collaboration with Bard College and MIT for APS's national physics outreach program.

Wigner & Racah Coefficients of U(4) and SU(3)

Main investigator · 2023–2024

Modernized the Draayer–Akiyama U(3) algorithm and developed the first general U(4) coupling implementation for nuclear modeling; released as the C++ libraries u3lib and u4lib.

Shape Isomers of α-like Nuclei

Main investigator · 2022–2023

Studied stable nuclear shapes, clustering, and collective behavior via the multiconfigurational dynamical symmetry (undergraduate thesis).

Orbital Regularity of Planetary Systems & the Kepler Problem

Co-investigator · 2021–2023

Explored hidden symmetries of the Kepler problem across large exoplanetary databases.
